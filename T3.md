## T3 boxes

When you have a corporate investment account holding an ETF which pays distributions (eg. XEI), you will see the following:
- Box 49: Actual amount of eligible dividends
- Box 21: Capital gains distribution
- Box 25: Foreign income
- Box 34: Foreign tax withheld
- Box 42: Return of capital (ROC)
- Box 26: Other income

## Matching ledger vs brokerage account
It's useful to be able to match the numbers in your ledger to what you see in your bank/brokerage statements.  
The investmnet distirbutions will arrive in your corporate investment account throughout the year, but we won't know the final tax classification until we get the T3 (note that sometimes the statement will call them "Dividends", but that doesn't mean that they are actually egilible dividends).  
You will typically receive a T3 in the 2nd half of March, and you might need a call to your brokerage to get them to email the T3 (and other) investment package PDFs.  
The adjusted cost base (ACB) is what you paid for an investment (average price per share = book cost / share quantity), and this amount changes when you buy/sell or when you get ROC (box 42) or capital gains (box 21) distributions.
When selling, capital gain (or loss if negative) = proceeds of disposion - book cost.

Different brokerages might handle ACB differently, all of them might occationally forget to property adjust it (e.g. with RBC if you sell the security before April) and sometimes report the wrong amount (which can be resolved by raising the issue with the brokerage), and they might even not update the ACB at all (ideally you should avoid these because it makes your life harder).  
For this reason, you will need to independently track the ACB in a spreadsheet, to properly report taxes and also to validate what you see from your brokerage.  
It's convenient to have your brokerage ledge account match what you see in your bank statements, so we will track the ACB it in the ledger.

Until we know what the real classification is, the distributions can be tracked in a temporary location:  
Revenue (GIFI 8299) / Investment revenue (GIFI 8090) / Interest from other Canadian sources (GIFI 8094) / TBD investment distributions (code 8094-2)  
In March when the T3 arrives, we can go back and reclassify to the precise tax treatment.  

In some cases the T3 that you receive will have slightly different amounts vs what is in your brokerage statements (typically by up to a few pennies and over time these differences should wash out).  
You need to report the amounts as they appear on the T3, not what is in your actual account, the difference can be tracked in:  
Revenue (GIFI 8299) / Investment revenue (code 8090-parent) / Investment revenue adjustment (GIFI 8090)
