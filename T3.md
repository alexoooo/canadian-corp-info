# T3 investment distributions handling for corporations

Who this is for: owners of Canadian Controller Private Corporation (CCPC), i.e. small business owners.  
If you have a corporate investment account (e.g. RBC direct investing), and you hold an ETF (eg. XEI), you will need to account for the T3 tax slip.  
Dates below are given assuming your company has a year end of December 31.

## T3 boxes

When you have a corporate investment account holding an ETF which pays distributions, you will see the following:
- Box 49: Actual amount of eligible dividends
- Box 21: Capital gains distribution
- Box 25: Foreign non-business income
- Box 34: Foreign tax withheld
- Box 42: Return of capital (ROC)
- Box 26: Other income


## Matching ledger vs brokerage account

Reconciling your numbers is easier when you can match the numbers in your ledger to what you see in your bank/brokerage statements.  
You will want to match the cash amount in your investment account, as well as the book cost of your investments.  

Book cost is what you paid for the investment, it increases when you buy and decreases when you sell.  
Market value is what you could currently sell the securities for, this value fluctuates and is not explicitly tracked in your ledger and is not filed as part of your taxes.  
Adjusted cost base (ACB) is the rolling volume weighted average price of your securities (book cost / share quantity).  
ACB can also change without you making any trades, decreasing with ROC (box 42) and increasing with capital gains distributions (box 21).  
Different brokerages might handle ACB differently, typically they will all handle buying/selling correctly, but they can differ in how they handle ROC and and capital gains distributions.  
All brokerages might occasionally forget to properly adjust the cost base (e.g. with RBC if you sell the security before April) and sometimes report the wrong amount (which can be resolved by raising the issue with the brokerage), and they might even not update the ACB at all (ideally you should avoid these because it makes your life harder).  
For this reason, you will need to independently track the ACB in a spreadsheet to properly report taxes and also to validate what you see from your brokerage.  

Investment distributions will arrive in your corporate investment account throughout the year, but we won't know the final tax classification until we get the T3 (note that sometimes the statement will call them "Dividends", but that doesn't mean that they are actually eligible dividends).  
You will typically receive a T3 in the 2nd half of March, and you might need a call to your brokerage to get them to email the T3 (and other) investment package PDFs.  
The numbers you see from your brokerage are not final, they can retroactively change with the handling of T3 slips and other adjustments, for this reason it's not possible to always match exactly.
You can match the numbers during the year, but then when you incorporate the T3 information this will change your numbers for the past year, and you will need a temporary plug to synchronize with the brokerage reporting.

It you are in doubt about the true tax treatment or you doubt what is in your T3, you can calculate the expected values based on the PDS provided by the Canadian Tax Breakdown Reporting Service from CDS:
https://ctbsext.posttrade.cds.ca/ctbsExt/external-landing

## Preliminary tracking throughout the year

Until we know what the real classification is, the distributions can be tracked in a temporary location:  
Revenue (GIFI 8299) / Investment revenue (GIFI 8090) / Interest from other Canadian sources (GIFI 8094) / TBD investment distributions (code 8094-2)  
In March when the T3 arrives, we can go back and reclassify to the precise tax treatment.  

In some cases the T3 that you receive will have slightly different amounts vs what is in your brokerage statements (typically by up to a few pennies and over time these differences should wash out).  
You need to report the amounts as they appear on the T3, not what is in your actual account, the difference can be tracked in:  
Revenue (GIFI 8299) / Investment revenue (code 8090-parent) / Investment revenue adjustment (GIFI 8090)  

You should be able to match the "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) and "Brokerage" (2303-1) accounts to what you see in the brokerage website and statements at any time throughout the year.  

These are the things that are useful to track separately from your ledger in a spreadsheet:  
- Cost base: trades (buy/sell) and ROC / capital gains dividends  
- Distributions: actual cash vs what's in the T3, and tax treatment (TBD initially and eventually corresponding to T3 boxes above)
- Paying dividends requires using GRIP/ERDTOH/NERDTOH which is handled by T2 software
- Capital dividend account: running total of all capital gains (including capital gains distributions), to be used for a tax efficient capital dividend  

## Eligible dividends - box 49
Debit: "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) within year, or "Dividends receivable" (1060) if declared in December but paid in Jan.  
Credit: "Dividends from Canadian sources" (GIFI 8096).  

Schedule 3 (S3) / Part 1 - Dividends received in tax year:  
- A = ETF fund name  
- Type of Column F = s.112  
- F = G = eligible dividend amount  

If you get paid eligible dividends (which already had income tax paid on them at the source), and you retain that and pay corporate income tax on it, and then pay that out to shareholders (who also pay personal tax on it).  
To avoid double-taxation (between the eligible dividend issuer, and the CCPC paying a dividend), the following are involved: GRIP (Schedule 53) / ERDTOH / NERDTOH  
- T2 / Page 7: Refundable dividend tax on hand  
- S3 / Page 3 / Part 3 / box 450 = Total taxable dividends paid in the taxation year to other than connected corporations  
- S3 / Page 3 / Part 4 / box 500 = "Total dividend paid in the tax year" (up to 450)  
Link: https://greenardgroup.com/refund-of-corporate-taxes-previously-paid/  


## Other income - box 26
Debit: "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) within year, or "Dividends receivable" (1060) if declared in December but paid in Jan.  
Credit: "Other investment income" (8094-1)


## Foreign income - box 25, and Foreign tax withheld - box 34
Debit box 25 amount - box 34 amount (net): "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) within year, or "Dividends receivable" (1060) if declared in December but paid in Jan.  
Debit box 34 amount: "Withholding taxes" (9283)  
Credit box 25 amount: "Dividends from foreign sources" (8097)  

Schedule 7 (S7) / Part 3 / box 019 - Total income from property from a source outside Canada (net of related expenses)  
^^ depending on your tax software (I use FutureTax T2), you can double click this box to get the following:  
S7WS - Schedule 7 worksheet / Page 1 / Income from property .. Foreign (column) / Other dividends  
Enter the box 25 amount, note that even though the section is called "net of related expenses", the full gross amount is entered.


## Return of capital (ROC) - box 42
This is the fund giving you back your own money, the investment value is reduced by the amount of cash that you receive, so this is not a "real" distribution.  

Debit: "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) within year, or "Dividends receivable" (1060) if declared in December but paid in Jan.  
Credit: "Brokerage" (2303-1), this reduces the value of your investment by the corresponding amount.  

When you retroactively classify a portion of the distribution as ROC, it involves reducing the value of your investment account, so the book cost will no longer match what was in your historical statements.  
In order for your "Brokerage" (2303-1) account to match what you see in your brokerage statements and website, you can add a temporary entry for the first date of the following year (e.g. Jan 1st):  
Debit: "Brokerage" (2303-1)  
Credit: "Brokerage - pending" (2303-2)  
Amount: sum of all ROC distributions  
Description: ROC pending brokerage update in April - to be reversed  

And then in April when your brokerage finally reflects this, you can reverse the above:
Debit: "Brokerage - pending" (2303-2)  
Credit: "Brokerage" (2303-1)  
Amount: sum of all ROC distributions  
Description: ROC pending brokerage update in April - reversal  


## Capital gains distribution - box 21

Box 21 comes in two flavours:
- Cash: which appears in your "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) account
- Phantom: non-cash amount, which is counted as a capital gain and increases ACB

Debit for box 21:
- Cash: "Deposits - investment" (1002-2), or "Dividends receivable" (1060) if declared in December but paid in Jan
- Phantom: "Brokerage" (2303-1)

Credit (in either case): "Capital gains distributions" (8211-2)  

When you enter the capital gains distribution, it increases the value of your investment account, the book cost will not match the end of year statement.  
Add a temporary entry for the first date of the following year (Jan 1st):  
Debit: "Brokerage - pending" (2303-2)  
Credit: "Brokerage" (2303-1)  
Description: phantom capital gains distribution pending brokerage update in April - to be reversed  

And then in April when your brokerage finally reflects this, reverse the above:
Debit: "Brokerage" (2303-1)  
Credit: "Brokerage - pending" (2303-2)  
Description: phantom capital gains distribution pending brokerage update in April - reversal  

In addition to the above ledger entries, enter the box 21 amount in the T2:
- Schedule 6, Part 4 (Summary of Dispositions of Capital Property / Other properties)  
  "Capital gains distribution - ETF trust T3 Box 21"  
  Report the amount as a capital gain, half of it will be taxed instead of the full amount
- Schedule 1, Page 4, Other deductions (Description 705 / Amount 395)  
  "Capital gains distribution T3 Box 21 - reported on Schedule 6"  
  Deduct it, to avoid double taxation from the fact that it's a capital gain and a revenue

Track the non-taxable portion (50%) of the box 21 amount in the Capital Dividend Account (CDA).
This is a spreadsheet that is not part of the ledger. It can be submitted as Schedule 89.
The CDA can be used to pay the tax-free portion of the capital gain from the corporation to the shareholders with a capital dividend.
It requires first verifying the CDA amount, possibly via physical mail to the CRA, which can be done once in a few years.
