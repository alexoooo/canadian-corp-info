# Documentation Audit

**Date**: 2026-05-28
**Scope**: All 20 Markdown content pages under `guide/`, plus `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, and
`CLAUDE.md`. This is a fresh full pass, not a follow-up. It builds on the four prior audits
(2026-04-28 → 2026-05-07) but its weight is on the large body of content added since the last
audit: the entire `guide/Cost-Recovery/` cluster (Cost-Recovery, Inventory, Capital-Cost-Allowance,
Materials-And-CIP) and the now-complete `Foreign-Currency.md` and `HST.md`, none of which existed
in finished form at the 2026-05-07 pass.
**Method**: Each page read in full. Every external citation was verified against the live source as
of the audit date — ITA/ETA sections and Regulations on laws-lois.justice.gc.ca, CRA forms/folios
/guides on canada.ca, the GIFI guide RC4088 (Rev.21), Bank of Canada, and the 2024 Fall Economic
Statement / Bill C-15 legislative record. Rates, thresholds, dollar caps, and dates were checked
against current 2026 values. Every CRITICAL/HIGH finding and every claimed citation discrepancy was
re-verified against the primary source before inclusion; several agent-reported candidates did not
survive that check and were dropped (see *What was NOT flagged*). Internal links and `#anchor`
targets were traced.

## Severity legend

- **CRITICAL** — factual error in tax/bookkeeping rules, or load-bearing instruction that will mislead a CCPC owner.
- **HIGH** — material clarity or consistency defect, or an in-scope concept the page promises to cover but doesn't.
- **MEDIUM** — improves accuracy or readability but not load-bearing.
- **LOW** — spelling, hyphenation, comma splices, single-word style.

## Capital-Cost-Allowance.md

### [CRITICAL] CCA-1 The Accelerated Investment Incentive is described under the superseded phase-out

`Capital-Cost-Allowance.md:11`, `:24`, and `:118-124` present AIIP as a 2018–2027 incentive that
"was subject to phase-out for property available for use after 2023" and whose "enhanced deduction
ends for property available for use after 2027." That was the law *before* the 2024 Fall Economic
Statement.

As of this audit the incentive has been reinstated and enacted:

- The 2024 Fall Economic Statement restored AIIP and full immediate expensing for manufacturing
  and processing equipment (Class 53), clean-energy equipment (Classes 43.1/43.2), and
  zero-emission vehicles (Classes 54/55/56), for property acquired on or after 2025-01-01 and
  available for use before 2030, with a new phase-out across 2030–2033.
- This was enacted by **Bill C-15 (Budget Implementation Act, 2025, No. 1), which received Royal
  Assent on 2026-03-26** and is in force as of this audit (verified against the 2024 FES tax-measures
  annex, the Finance Canada legislative-passage notice, and the Parliament of Canada Royal Assent
  record).

Effect: a reader who takes the page at face value will believe enhanced/immediate first-year CCA
is no longer available and will under-claim, overpaying tax. The page is correctly marked
`AI GENERATED, REVIEW IN PROGRESS`, and its worked examples are safely hedged ("AIIP enhanced
first-year deduction may apply for 2026 acquisitions; verify the current multiplier on CRA's AIIP
page" — `:271`, `:321`), so the examples themselves are not wrong. But the narrative statements at
`:11`, `:24`, and `:118-124`, and the matching `Glossary.md:11` entry ("available for use before
2028"), state superseded law as current and must be rewritten to the reinstated rules. This is the
single most important content fix in the repo.

### [MEDIUM] CCA-2 Four wrong GIFI fixed-asset codes in the bookkeeping section and worked examples

`Capital-Cost-Allowance.md:230` maps "`Computer equipment` 1770, `Furniture and fixtures` 1780,
`Motor vehicles` 1740, `Machinery and equipment` 1900, `Goodwill / intangibles` 1880-series", and
the worked examples reuse some of these (`:261`, `:289` computer equipment 1770; `:345` goodwill
1880-series). Verified against RC4088 (Rev.21):

- `1770` is **Small tools**, not computer equipment. Computer equipment/software is **1774**.
- `1780` is **Earth stations**, not furniture and fixtures. Furniture and fixtures is **1787**.
- `1740` is the generic parent group (machinery/equipment/furniture/fixtures); **Motor vehicles is 1742**.
- There is **no 18xx series**; goodwill is **2012** and the intangible-assets parent is **2010**.
- `1900` ("Other tangible capital assets") is valid; the "Machinery and equipment" gloss is loose but acceptable.

The same `1770`-for-computer-equipment error is echoed in `HST.md:324` (and the example carries
through `HST.md:372`). A reader copying these codes onto Schedule 100 mis-classifies the asset.
Correct the five codes in both files.

### [MEDIUM] CCA-3 Internal contradiction on the Class 13 half-year rule

`Capital-Cost-Allowance.md:56` says "*Class 13* … leasehold improvements; **half-year applies**".
`:116` lists Class 13 among the classes **exempt** from the half-year rule. `:116` is correct: the
Regulation 1100(2) half-year adjustment explicitly excludes Class 13 (its element F lists Classes
13, 14, 15, 23, 24, 27, 29, 34, 52, 54–56). Class 13 CCA is computed under Schedule III /
para 1100(1)(b), which has its own first-year limitation that produces a half-year-*like* effect —
which is likely why `:56` says "half-year applies" — but the statutory half-year rule does not
apply to it. Fix `:56` to state Class 13 is exempt from the 1100(2) rule and note the Schedule III
first-year limit instead; this aligns `:56` with `:116` and with `Glossary.md:25`.

### [LOW] CCA-4 Minor citation and precision items

- `:397` Citations lists "special rule for Class 10.1 (s.13(2))". The Class 10.1 no-recapture/half-CCA
  treatment runs through s.20(16.1) and Regulation 1100(2.5) (already cited at `:173`); the s.13(2)
  pointer is imprecise.
- `:126` and `:200` state that "a Dec 2024 amendment removed the short-fiscal-year proration for
  DIEP." This is correct (Canada Gazette, 2024-12-04, retroactive to tax years ending on or after
  2021-04-19), but it removed the *second* (deduction-level) proration only; the $1.5M immediate-
  expensing limit itself still prorates for a short year under Regulation 1104(3.5)(b). Phrase so it
  does not read as "DIEP no longer prorates at all."

## HST.md

### [HIGH] HST-1 Input-tax-credit documentary thresholds are outdated

`HST.md:158-160` (and the Citations note at `:455`) give the prescribed-information bands as:

> *Under $30*: supplier name, transaction date, total amount paid
> *$30 to $149.99*: the above plus the supplier's GST/HST registration number …
> *$150 or more*: the above plus the recipient's name, terms of payment, and a description …

Budget 2021 raised these thresholds effective 2021-04-20. The current Input Tax Credit Information
(GST/HST) Regulations (SOR/91-45), s.3, use **$100** and **$500** (confirmed verbatim against the
regulation): under $100 → basic information; $100 to under $500 → add the supplier's registration
number; $500 or more → add the recipient's name, terms of payment, and a description of each supply.
The structure the page describes is right; only the two dollar boundaries are wrong. (Following the
stale lower thresholds is over-cautious rather than dangerous, but the figures are stated as current
law with a citation, so they should be corrected at `:158-160` and `:455`.)

### [MEDIUM] HST-2 Capital-property ITC: 10%–90% proration vs the >50% all-or-nothing rule

`HST.md:166` says, under denied/limited ITCs:

> *Personal-use property*: ITC denied where business use is 10% or less; pro-rated where business
> use is between 10% and 90% on capital property

But `:174-175` and `:253-254` correctly give the rule that actually applies to a CCPC's capital
*personal* property: business use over 50% → full ITC; 50% or less → none (the all-or-nothing test
in ETA s.199). The 10%/90% proration is the capital-*real*-property pattern (percentage of
commercial use, with the 90%/10% rounding in s.141), not the capital-personal-property rule for a
corporation. As written, `:166` reads as if a vehicle or computer at 60% business use gives a
prorated ITC, which contradicts `:174-175`. Distinguish the property types, or drop the 10%–90%
line for the personal-property audience.

### [LOW] HST-3 Memorandum 14 citation is mismatched (already in the page TODO)

`HST.md:461` cites "GST/HST Memorandum 14 Special Quick Method" but points at a `…/19-1.html` URL,
and the Special Quick Method is for public-service bodies, not an owner-managed CCPC. The page's own
TODO (`:484`) already flags this; either correct the reference or remove it.

## Foreign-Currency.md

### [MEDIUM] FC-1 Self-contradictory FX-date sentence for the Norbert's Gambit ACB

`Foreign-Currency.md:229`:

> The ACB of the DLR.U leg is the CAD equivalent of the USD purchase price at the *settlement-date*
> BoC rate (consistent with the trade-date convention used elsewhere in this guide for securities;
> CRA technical interpretation 2015-0588981C6 confirms the settlement-date rate for ACB on Gambit)

This says **settlement-date** and then claims consistency with the **trade-date** convention — the
two cannot both be true. The page's own worked example uses the **trade-date** rate ("USD 10,009.95
× 1.36 = CAD 13,613.53 … at trade-date BoC 1.36", `:260`, `:283`), and the rest of the guide's
load-bearing convention (`AGENTS.md`, `Adjusted-Cost-Base.md:106`) is trade-date FX for purchases
and sales. Two things to fix:

1. The sentence is internally inconsistent — pick trade-date (to match the example and the guide's
   convention) and drop "settlement-date", or vice-versa, but not both.
2. The cited CRA technical interpretation 2015-0588981C6 is real (it exists as an APFF Roundtable
   item, 2015-10-09) and does point to the **settlement-date** rate for FX on dispositions — which
   is in genuine tension with the guide's stated trade-date convention. It is also a general
   securities/FX statement, not a DLR/DLR.U-specific ruling, and is a transcribed verbal position
   (persuasive, not binding). The maintainer should decide whether the guide stays on trade-date
   (and cites the TI only as the alternative view) or acknowledges the settlement-date position
   here; either way `:229` should stop asserting both at once.

## Shareholder-Dividends.md

### [MEDIUM] SD-1 Part III.1 30% rate — wrong trigger

`Shareholder-Dividends.md:411`:

> The Part III.1 tax under ITA s.185.1 is 20% of the excess, raised to 30% if the designation was
> made knowingly or in circumstances amounting to gross negligence.

A 30% rate does exist (20% under s.185.1(1)(a) plus an additional 10% under s.185.1(1)(b)), so the
20% figure is not the whole story — but the trigger is wrong. The extra 10% applies where the
excessive eligible dividend designation arises under paragraph (c) of the s.89(1) definition — the
anti-avoidance rule for transactions one of whose main purposes is to **artificially inflate GRIP or
deflate LRIP**. It is an objective GRIP/LRIP-manipulation test, not the "knowingly or gross
negligence" culpability standard (that wording belongs to the s.163(2) penalty regime). Also worth
adding: the s.185.1(2) joint election to reclassify the excess as a non-eligible dividend is *not*
available when the 30% rate applies.

### [MEDIUM] SD-2 PUC-reduction deemed dividend cites the public-corporation provision

`Shareholder-Dividends.md:51` (and Citations `:612`, `:623`) say a distribution that reduces the
PUC of a **private** corporation "is *deemed* to be a dividend under ITA s.84(4.1)". The provisions
are reversed: **s.84(4)** is the private-corporation PUC-reduction rule; **s.84(4.1)** is the
**public**-corporation rule (verified against the section text). Change s.84(4.1) → s.84(4) in all
three places.
