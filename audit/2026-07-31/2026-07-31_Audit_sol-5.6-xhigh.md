## Severity Legend

- **CRITICAL** — factual error in tax/bookkeeping rules, or load-bearing instruction that will mislead a CCPC owner.
- **HIGH** — material clarity or consistency defect, or an in-scope concept the page promises to cover but doesn't.
- **MEDIUM** — improves accuracy or readability but not load-bearing.
- **LOW** — spelling, hyphenation, comma splices, single-word style.

## TL;DR

## Repo-Wide Findings

### [CRITICAL] R-1 Capital dividends are mixed into the taxable-dividend reporting path

**Files**: `guide/Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md:63-67,90-107,347-354`;
`guide/Overview/Concept-Map.md:41-45`  
**Quoted text**: “`T2054 --> SH`”; “Trigger a *T5* slip”; “Each flavour has its own gross-up and DTC”;
“`T5 / T2054 to shareholder`.”  
**Problem**: a capital dividend elected under ITA s.83(2) is excluded from a resident shareholder's income. It has
no T5, gross-up, or dividend tax credit. Form T2054 is the private corporation's election filed with CRA, not a
shareholder slip. Some nearby prose is correct, so the diagrams and generic dividend bullets directly contradict it.  
**Primary sources**: [ITA s.83(2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-83.html);
[Regulations s.2101](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.%2C_c._945/section-2101.html);
[CRA T2054](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t2054.html);
[CRA T5 exclusions](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/financial-slips-summaries/return-investment-income-t5/t5-slip/when-you-have-prepare-a-t5-slip-1.html).  
**Suggested fix**: split taxable dividends (T5, gross-up, DTC) from capital dividends (resolution/payment plus the
corporation's T2054 filing), and correct both diagrams.

### [CRITICAL] R-2 Non-resident status is treated as sufficient for zero-rating

**Files**: `guide/Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md:445-448`;
`guide/Bookkeeping/Foreign-Currency/Foreign-Currency.md:22-23`  
**Quoted text**: “Sales to non-resident customers ... are *zero-rated*”; “Invoices to a non-resident US customer for
services are *zero-rated* GST/HST.”  
**Problem**: non-residence alone does not zero-rate a supply. The exported-goods or exported-services provision must
apply, place-of-supply rules still matter, and Schedule VI Part V contains express exclusions. For example, general
s.7 excludes advisory, consulting, and professional services, which must qualify under a different provision such
as s.23. The unconditional shortcut can cause HST undercollection.  
**Primary sources**: [ETA Schedule VI, Part V](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-15/section-sched201390.html);
[CRA Memorandum 4-5-3](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-5-3/exports-services-intangible-personal-property.html).  
**Suggested fix**: say that many exports are zero-rated only when the applicable statutory provision and all its
conditions are satisfied; route service invoices through the detailed s.7/s.23 and place-of-supply test.

### [HIGH] R-5 Retained earnings is treated as spendable cash

### [HIGH] R-6 The three-month T2 balance-due test is incomplete and keyed to the wrong year

**Files**: `guide/Filing-And-CRA/Payment/Payment.md:27-31`,
`guide/Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md:476`, `guide/Overview/Concept-Map.md:334-340`,
`guide/Corporate-Lifecycle/Starting-Up.md:174-178`  
**Quoted text**: “Three months for a CCPC that claims the small business deduction with taxable income within the
business limit”; a first-year balance is “generally due at three months.”  
**Problem**: the general balance-due day is two months. The three-month rule requires CCPC status throughout the
current year, an SBD deduction in the current or preceding year, and a separate preceding-year taxable-income test,
including the associated-group formulation. Current-year income within the business limit is not the test, and a
new corporation does not automatically qualify.  
**Primary sources**: [ITA s.248(1), “balance-due day”](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-248.html);
[CRA balance-due day](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/corporations/corporation-payments/paying-your-balance-corporation-tax/balance-day.html).  
**Suggested fix**: use two months as the safe default and set out the complete four-part/associated-group test before
using three months.

### [HIGH] R-7 Current Ontario SBD examples do not apply the July 1, 2026 transition

**Files**: `guide/Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md:273-279`,
`guide/Overview/Tax-Integration.md:83-105,130-133`,
`guide/Paying-Yourself/Dividends/Dividends-Examples.md:22-33,76-104`,

### [MEDIUM] R-8 CCA is described as causing a home-office change in use

**Quoted text**: “CCA converts that portion to income-producing property”; “CCA is the step that converts the

**Suggested fix**: say that CCA disqualifies the administrative concession and can cause CRA to apply the
partial-change rule from the earlier income-use date.

### [MEDIUM] GL-1 “Available for use” is reduced to first use and an imprecise long-stop

### [MEDIUM] FR-1 The life-insurance CDA credit is not a “non-taxable portion” of the payout

**File**: `guide/Overview/Further-Reading.md:26-31`  
**Quote**: “The non-taxable portion of a life-insurance payout to the corporation credits the capital dividend account.”  
**Explanation**: the CDA addition is generally the life-insurance proceeds received because of death minus the policy's adjusted cost basis immediately before death, subject to the statutory adjustments.
