# T2125 and Expenses

**Who this is for**:
- Sole proprietors preparing a T2125, or working out the mixed-use expense claims (home, vehicle, phone)
- CCPC owners comparing against the corporate reimbursement and benefit rules

**TLDR**:
- Form T2125 (*Statement of Business or Professional Activities*) is the business statement inside your T1
- The deduction gates are the same as the corporate ones: incurred to earn income (s.18(1)(a)) and reasonable (s.67)
- Business-use-of-home is the big personal-overlap claim: s.18(12)-gated, prorated by area, unable to create a loss
  - Unlike the corporate reimbursement route, it includes the business share of mortgage interest and property tax
- Vehicle and other mixed-use assets are claimed at the business-use share, with CCA under the same class system

Limitations:
- One business per T2125; a second business files its own statement
- Professional-practice WIP elections and partnerships are out of scope
- Dollar caps are the 2026 amounts; CRA resets the vehicle limits annually
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## The T2125 Inside the T1

One T2125 per business, filed with the T1; the net income flows to the T1's self-employment income line.  
The form's parts: identification (with an industry code), income, expenses, business-use-of-home, and the CCA charts.  

- Gross income is what your books say; T4A box 048 totals reconcile to it rather than define it
- If you are GST/HST-registered and claim ITCs, record expenses net of the recovered tax
  - The ITC side is worked in [HST for Sole Proprietors](HST-For-Sole-Proprietors.md#mixed-use-itcs)
- The filing and payment clocks (June 15 filing, April 30 balance) are worked in [CPP and the T1](CPP-And-The-T1.md#the-june-15-deadline)


## What You Can Deduct

The same two gates as the corporate pages:
- Incurred to earn income from the business (ITA [s.18(1)(a)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-18.html))
- Reasonable in the circumstances (ITA [s.67](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-67.html))

The recurring lines for a service business:
- Supplies, software subscriptions, professional fees, insurance, advertising, bank charges on the business account
- Phone and internet at the documented business-use share; the personal share is never deductible
- Meals and entertainment at 50% (ITA [s.67.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-67.1.html))

The classification instincts from [Expense Classification](../Bookkeeping/Expense-Classification.md) carry over.  
The capitalize-vs-expense line does too: a long-lived purchase goes through CCA rather than the year's expenses.  


## Business-Use-of-Home

The gate is ITA [s.18(12)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-18.html): the work space must meet one of two branches:
- It is your *principal place of business*, or
- It is used *exclusively* to earn business income, *and* regularly and continuously for meeting clients, customers, or patients

The exclusivity condition attaches to the second branch only; a principal place of business need not be exclusive.  
A consultant working mainly from a home desk meets the first branch.  

The proportion:
- The work space's share of the home's area
- Where the space also serves personal use, prorate further by hours of business use

Eligible costs, at the business-use proportion:
- Rent (as a tenant), utilities, home insurance, maintenance
- Property tax and mortgage *interest* (never principal)
  - This is wider than the corporate reimbursement route, which excludes mortgage interest and property tax
  - The corporate side is worked in [Owner-Corporation Transactions - Home Office](../Paying-Yourself/Owner-Corporation-Transactions.md#home-office)

The no-loss rule (s.18(12)(b) and (c)):
- Home-office costs cannot create or increase a business loss
- The denied amount carries forward indefinitely and deducts in a later profitable year, under the same limit

The principal-residence caution is the same as the corporate page's:
- Do not claim CCA on the home-office portion (CRA Folio S1-F3-C2)
- CCA converts that portion to income-producing property and risks the *principal residence exemption* on a later sale
- The exemption and the change-in-use rules are worked in [Home Office and the Principal Residence](../Personal-Tax/Home-Office-And-Principal-Residence.md#change-of-use-and-the-cca-trap)


## Vehicle

- Claim the business-use proportion of the year's costs, from a contemporaneous km log (business km ÷ total km)
- Prorated costs: fuel, insurance, licence, maintenance, lease payments or loan interest, CCA
- 2026 caps for a passenger vehicle:
  - Class 10.1 capital cost ceiling: $39,000 before tax (Reg 7307; the class split is worked in [CCA Classification](../Operations/Cost-Recovery/Capital-Cost-Allowance/CCA-Classification.md))
  - Deductible lease cost: $1,100 per month before tax
  - Deductible loan interest: $350 per month
- Commuting is personal
  - Travel from a home office that is the principal place of business to a client site is business travel (see TODO)


## Capital Assets and CCA

The CCA machinery is identical to the corporate pages: classes, rates, the half-year and AIIP rules, UCC, recapture.  
The concepts are worked in [Capital Cost Allowance](../Operations/Cost-Recovery/Capital-Cost-Allowance/Capital-Cost-Allowance.md).  

The sole-prop differences:
- Claims go on the T2125's own CCA charts, not Schedule 8 of a T2
- A mixed-use asset enters at the business-use share of its cost
  - A later change in the business-use proportion triggers the s.13(7) change-of-use adjustments
- CCA remains discretionary: claim less than the maximum in a low-income year and preserve UCC
