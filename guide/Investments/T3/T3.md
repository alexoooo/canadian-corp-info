# T3 Investment Distributions Handling for Corporations

**Who this is for**:
- Owners of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) who get a T3 from stock or ETF investments

You might have a corporate investment account (e.g. RBC Direct Investing) holding an ETF (e.g. XEI).  
If so, you will need to account for the T3 tax slip.  

This document covers T3 slips issued by Canadian trust-based ETFs and similar trust investments.  
ETFs or funds structured as corporations generally report through a [T5](../T5/T5.md) and are outside the scope of this page.  
There are other T slips to consider, such as T5 and T5008.  
Those have their own tax treatment (outside the scope of this document).  
This document is aimed at Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) claiming the small business deduction (SBD).  
The SBD business limit is $500,000 and this guide assumes active business income is under that limit.  
Using a Dividend Reinvestment Plan (DRIP) increases paperwork and complexity; this document assumes DRIP is not used.  
Dates below are given assuming your company has a year-end of December 31.  
Tax information can change over time.  
For example, the capital gains inclusion rate was going to increase to 2/3, before the proposal was cancelled.  
The following is my understanding as of 2026.  


## T3 Boxes

When you have a corporate investment account holding an ETF which pays distributions, you will see the following:
- Box 49: Actual amount of eligible dividends: [T3 Box 49 - Eligible Dividends](T3-Box-49-Eligible-Dividends.md)
- Box 21: Capital gains distribution: [T3 Box 21 - Capital Gains Distribution](T3-Box-21-Capital-Gains-Distribution.md)
- Box 23: Actual amount of non-eligible dividends (e.g. XCV in 2024)
  - See [T3 Box 23 - Non-Eligible Dividends](T3-Box-23-Non-Eligible-Dividends.md)
- Box 25: Foreign non-business income (foreign interest or foreign dividends)
  - See [T3 - Box 25 Foreign Income / Box 34 Foreign Tax Withheld](T3_Box-25-Foreign-Income_Box-34-Foreign-Tax-Withheld.md)
- Box 34: Foreign non-business income tax paid: [T3 - Box 25 Foreign Income / Box 34 Foreign Tax Withheld](T3_Box-25-Foreign-Income_Box-34-Foreign-Tax-Withheld.md)
- Box 42: Amount resulting in cost base adjustment, typically a downward adjustment, most often Return of Capital (ROC)
  - See [T3 Box 42 - Return of Capital](T3-Box-42-Return-Of-Capital.md)
- Box 26: Other income: [T3 - Box 26 Other Income](T3-Box-26-Other-Income.md)

Each sub-page has the detailed treatment, the ledger entries, and the T2 schedule mapping for its box.  
This page keeps the shared machinery: the account tree, the ledger-vs-brokerage matching workflow, the
preliminary tracking convention, and the per-box T2 summary.

## Matching Ledger vs Brokerage Account

Reconciling is easier when you can match the numbers in your ledger to your bank/brokerage statements.  
You will want to match the cash amount in your investment account, as well as the book cost of your investments.  
Matching exactly is not always possible, but typically you can achieve this by carefully managing your ledger accounts.  

Book Cost (Book Value): amount shown on your investment statements.  
It typically includes your initial investment, increases when you buy, and decreases when you sell.  
Adjusted Cost Base (ACB): official tax term used by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).  
It is used to calculate capital gains or losses when you sell an asset.  
Ideally these two would be identical, but in practice you might see brokerage-dependent differences with the following:
- Phantom distributions, cost base adjustments, and re-invested distributions
  - Phantom distributions: non-cash portion of Box 21 from ETF where no new units are issued
  - Cost base adjustments: box 42, typically ROC
  - Some brokers such as RBC Direct Investing do handle these, but possibly delayed until April
    - In some cases the handling is missing entirely
  - Other brokers may not handle some or all of these at all, the behaviour can be inconsistent
- External holdings (same security in different account) may not be correctly tracked by your brokerage
  - The same applies to [superficial loss](../Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base.md#capital-loss-carry-forward--back--superficial-loss) rules

All brokerages might occasionally forget to properly adjust the Book Cost.  
For example with RBC if you sell the security before April.  
They might also report the wrong amount, which can usually be resolved by raising the issue with the brokerage.  
For this reason, you will need to independently track the ACB in a spreadsheet.  
That lets you properly report taxes and also validate what you see from your brokerage.  

Investment distributions will arrive in your corporate investment account throughout the year.  
We won't know the final tax classification until we get the T3.  
Sometimes the statement will call them "Dividends", but that doesn't mean they are actually eligible dividends.  
You will typically receive a T3 in the second half of March.  
You might need a call to your brokerage to get them to email the T3 (and other) investment package PDFs.  
The numbers you see from your brokerage are not final.  
They can retroactively change with the handling of T3 slips and other adjustments.  
You can match the numbers during the year, before the T3 information is incorporated.  
Once you incorporate the T3 information this will change your numbers for the past year.  
You will need a temporary plug to synchronize with the brokerage reporting.  

You might be in doubt about the true tax treatment, or doubt what is in your T3.  
You can calculate the expected values based on the PDF provided by the Canadian Tax Breakdown Reporting Service (CTBS).  
CTBS is from CDS (Canadian Depository for Securities):  
https://ctbsext.posttrade.cds.ca/ctbsExt/external-landing


## Preliminary Tracking Throughout the Year

Until we know what the real classification is, the distributions can be tracked in a temporary location:  
Revenue (GIFI 8299) / Investment revenue (code 8090-parent) /  
&ensp; Investment revenue - detail accounts (GIFI 8090) / TBD investment distributions (code 8090-3)  
In March when the T3 arrives, we go back and reclassify to the precise tax treatment.  
As an accounting convention, the prior entries can be edited without explicit reversal.  

In some cases the T3 that you receive will have slightly different amounts vs what is in your brokerage statements.  
Typically the difference is up to a few pennies, and over time these differences should wash out.  
You need to report the amounts as they appear on the T3, not what is in your actual account.  
The difference can be tracked in:  
Revenue (GIFI 8299) / Investment revenue (code 8090-parent) /  
&ensp; Investment revenue - detail accounts (GIFI 8090) / Investment revenue adjustment (code 8090-1)  

This accounting convention is a pragmatic trade-off.  
Before the retroactive T3 adjustments arrive, your ledger accounts match the brokerage statements exactly.  
The matching accounts: "Deposits - investment" (1002-2) and "Brokerage" (2303-1).  
After the retroactive adjustments are made, the past-year brokerage statements will be out of sync with your ledger.  
Maintaining both views simultaneously is possible but adds complexity.  
Depending on the brokerage, the adjustment might be propagated back to historical records.  
It might instead only appear going forward.  

These are the things that are useful to track separately from your ledger in a spreadsheet:  
- Cost base: trades (buy/sell) and ROC / capital gains dividends
- Distributions: actual cash vs what's in the T3, and tax treatment
  - TBD initially and eventually corresponding to T3 boxes above
- Paying dividends: involves GRIP, ERDTOH, and NERDTOH (mechanics covered in [ERDTOH-NERDTOH.md](../../Paying-Yourself/Dividends/ERDTOH-NERDTOH.md)); handled by T2 software
- Capital dividend account (CDA): a tax-efficient way to pay dividends from capital gains
  - See [Capital-Dividend-Account.md](../Capital-Dividend-Account/Capital-Dividend-Account.md)


## T2 Reporting Summary

Where each box lands on the T2; the entries themselves are worked on the per-box sub-pages:

| Box | Income character | T2 reporting | Pool effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 49 | Eligible dividend | S3 Part 1; s.112 deduction (line 320) | Part IV 38⅓% → ERDTOH; adds to GRIP |
| 23 | Non-eligible dividend | S3 Part 1; s.112 deduction (line 320) | Part IV 38⅓% → NERDTOH |
| 26 | Other income (property income) | S125 revenue; S7 Part 1 Box 032 | AII; refundable tax → NERDTOH |
| 25 | Foreign non-business income | S7 (Boxes 032 and 019); S1 add-back of Box 34 | AII; refundable tax → NERDTOH |
| 34 | Foreign tax withheld | S21 foreign non-business tax credit | — |
| 42 | Return of capital | No current entry; reduces ACB (later S6 gain) | — |
| 21 | Capital gains distribution | S6 Part 4; S1 line 113 and deduction; S7 | Taxable half → AII; half → CDA |

Dividends enter at the actual amount (Boxes 49 and 23); the T1-side gross-up boxes are ignored.  
The Part IV rate is 38⅓% on dividends received from non-connected corps under ITA [s.186(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-186.html).  
The pool is ERDTOH or NERDTOH per the type of dividend received under ITA [s.129(4)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-129.html).  
For pool definitions, the dividend-refund formula on payout, and the year-end ordering rule, see [ERDTOH and NERDTOH](../../Paying-Yourself/Dividends/ERDTOH-NERDTOH.md).  
It also includes a fill-side worked example.  

See: CRA T2 Corporation Income Tax Guide (T4012): https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4012.html
