# T5 Investment Income Handling for Corporations

**Who this is for**:
- Owners of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC)
- Receiving a T5 from investments held in a corporate account

A corporate investment account (e.g. RBC Direct Investing) produces a *T5 Statement of Investment Income* for:
- A dividend-paying Canadian stock
- A corporate-class fund
- A GIC
- A bank/broker high-interest savings deposit (an Investment Savings Account, ISA)

The slip type follows the issuer's legal structure, not the kind of income:
- A *trust* reports through a T3; see [T3](../T3/T3.md)
  - Most Canadian index ETFs and high-interest savings (HISA) ETFs are structured as mutual fund trusts
- A *corporation* (a stock, a mutual fund corporation, a corporate-class fund) reports through a T5
- A *securities disposition* (you sell a holding) reports through a T5008; see [T5008](../T5008/T5008.md)

This page covers the T5 your corporation *receives* on its investments.  
The T5 your corporation *issues* to its shareholder when it pays a taxable dividend is a separate obligation.  
That side is covered in [Bookkeeping and information slips](../../Paying-Yourself/Dividends/Bookkeeping-And-Slips.md).  

Limitations:
- Covers a CCPC claiming the small business deduction (SBD)
  - With active business income under the $500,000 business limit
- Covers plain investment holdings (stocks, corporate-class funds, GICs, high-interest savings/ISA deposits)
  - Annuities, insurance products, and structured products beyond equity-linked notes are out of scope
- Dates assume a December 31 year-end
- Tax information can change over time
  - One example: the capital gains inclusion rate was going to increase to 2/3, before the proposal was cancelled
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## T5 Boxes

Boxes on a T5 from a corporation-issued holding:
- Box 24: Actual amount of eligible dividends
- Box 10: Actual amount of dividends other than eligible (non-eligible)
- Box 13: Interest from Canadian sources
- Box 14: Other income from Canadian sources
- Box 15: Foreign income
- Box 16: Foreign tax paid
- Box 17: Royalties from Canadian sources
- Box 18: Capital gains dividends (see [T5 - Box 18 Capital Gains Dividends](T5-Box-18-Capital-Gains-Dividends.md))
- Box 30: Equity linked notes interest; treat it as Box 13 interest
  - A linked note: a market-linked GIC or principal-protected note
  - On sale or maturity, ITA [s.20(14.2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-20.html) deems the return-based portion of the proceeds to be accrued interest

Boxes the corporation does not act on:
- Box 11, Box 12, Box 25, Box 26: the grossed-up *taxable amount* and the *dividend tax credit*
  - For non-eligible (11/12) and eligible (25/26) dividends
  - Individual-T1 figures; a corporation ignores them (see [Corporation vs personal handling](#corporation-vs-personal-handling))
- Box 27: foreign-currency indicator (e.g. USD); not income
  - It flags that the boxes are stated in a foreign currency and must be converted to CAD
  - See [Foreign Currency](../../Bookkeeping/Foreign-Currency/Foreign-Currency.md)
- Box 21, Box 22, Box 23, Box 28, Box 29: report code and recipient identification; no tax treatment
  - The identification: account, transit, recipient type
- Box 19 (accrued income: annuities): out of scope for this audience


## Corporation vs Personal Handling

Most T5 guidance online is written for an individual filing a T1.  
There a Canadian dividend is grossed up and then offset by a dividend tax credit.  
A corporation does none of that.  

For a dividend received from a Canadian corporation:
- Include the *actual* amount only (Box 10 or Box 24) in income under ITA [s.82(1)(a)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-82.html)
  - The gross-up (s.82(1)(b)) and the dividend tax credit ([s.121](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-121.html)) are available to individuals only
- Deduct the actual amount again under [s.112](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-112.html), removing the dividend from Part I taxable income
- Pay Part IV tax of 38⅓% on the *actual* amount (Box 10 or Box 24), not the grossed-up Box 11/25 amount

The corporation uses Box 10 and Box 24, and leaves Box 11, Box 12, Box 25, and Box 26 untouched.  

How each box maps to a schedule and a tax pool:

## Matching Ledger vs Brokerage Account

Investment income arrives in your corporate account throughout the year.  
The final tax classification is not known until the T5 arrives, typically in February or March.  
A statement line labelled "dividend" is not necessarily an eligible dividend.  
It may turn out to be interest, foreign income, or a capital gains dividend.  
Park amounts in a temporary account during the year and reclassify once the slip confirms the split.  
The reconciliation approach is the same as for T3; see [Matching ledger vs brokerage account](../T3/T3.md#matching-ledger-vs-brokerage-account).  

If the T5 amounts differ slightly from your booked figures, report the amounts as they appear on the T5.  
Carry the difference in `Investment revenue adjustment` (8090-1).

## Eligible Dividends - Box 24

Debit: `Deposits - investment` (1002-2).  
Credit: `Dividends from Canadian sources` (8096).  
If declared in December but paid in January, debit `Investment distributions receivable` (1060-1) instead.  

Report on Schedule 3 (S3 - *Dividends Received, Taxable Dividends Paid, and Part IV Tax Calculation*).  
Part 1 (dividends received in the tax year) entries:
- A = payer name (e.g. the corporation or fund that issued the T5)
- Type of Column F = s.112
- F = G = actual eligible dividend amount (Box 24)

Part IV tax of 38⅓% on the received dividend populates ERDTOH (ITA [s.186(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-186.html)).  
The rate is 38⅓% for non-connected payers (s.186(1)(a)); connected payers are a flow-through (s.186(1)(b)).  
The s.112 deduction is taken on line 320 of the T2 to remove the dividend from Part I taxable income.  
This is mechanically identical to [T3 eligible dividends - Box 49](../T3/T3.md#eligible-dividends---box-49).  
For the pool definitions and the refund on payout, see [ERDTOH and NERDTOH](../../Paying-Yourself/Dividends/ERDTOH-NERDTOH.md).  


## Non-Eligible Dividends - Box 10

Debit: `Deposits - investment` (1002-2).  
Credit: `Dividends from Canadian sources - non-eligible` (8096-1).  
If declared in December but paid in January, debit `Investment distributions receivable` (1060-1) instead.  

Report on S3 Part 1 the same way as Box 24, using the actual amount (Box 10).  
The s.112 deduction still applies.  
Part IV tax of 38⅓% on a non-eligible dividend flows into NERDTOH rather than ERDTOH.  


## Interest - Box 13

Debit: `Deposits - investment` (1002-2).  
Credit: `Interest from other Canadian sources` (8094).  
If accrued at year-end but paid in January, debit `Investment distributions receivable` (1060-1) instead.  

Interest is property income and forms part of Aggregate Investment Income (AII), entered on Schedule 7 (S7).  
A corporation reports interest on an annual accrual basis under ITA [s.12(3)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-12.html).  
For a multi-year compound GIC or note that pays at maturity, accrue and report each year.  
No cash and no T5 arrive until maturity.  
For a monthly-paying ISA deposit the accrual and the cash effectively coincide, so the timing difference rarely matters.  
Box 30 (equity linked notes interest) is the same income character.  
It posts to the same `Interest from other Canadian sources` (8094) account.  

### Multi-Year GICs, Accrued but Unpaid

A compound GIC pays nothing until maturity, and the tax does not wait.  
Under ITA [s.12(3)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-12.html) a corporation includes the interest that accrues to the end of *each* taxation year.  
(The *anniversary day* rule in s.12(4) is the individuals' regime; a corporation accrues to its own year-end.)  

A 3-year $50,000 compound GIC at 4%, bought Oct 1, December year-end:

Year 1, Dec 31 — accrue 92 days of interest ($50,000 × 4% × 92⁄365 ≈ $504):
- Debit `Accrued investment income` (1482) = $504
- Credit `Interest from other Canadian sources` (8094) = $504

Year 2, Oct 1 — the issuer credits the first year's $2,000 into the GIC balance:
- Debit `Deposits - investment` (1002-2) = $2,000
- Credit `Accrued investment income` (1482) = $504
- Credit `Interest from other Canadian sources` (8094) = $1,496

Year 2, Dec 31 — accrue 92 days on the grown balance, and so on to maturity.  

Each year's `8094` total flows to Schedule 7 as that year's interest income.  
A T5 arriving at maturity (or on anniversary years) reports interest the books have largely already recognized.  
Tie the slip to the accrual record and report only what has not been included before — the slip timing never adds income twice.  


## Other Income - Box 14

Debit: `Deposits - investment` (1002-2).  
Credit: `Other investment income` (8090-2).  
If declared in December but paid in January, debit `Investment distributions receivable` (1060-1) instead.  

Box 14 is a residual bucket of Canadian-source property income, included in AII on Schedule 7.  
It is the T5 analogue of T3 Box 26.  
For the AII classification, the NERDTOH link, and the SBD grind over $50,000 of AAII, see [T3 - Box 26 Other Income](../T3/T3-Box-26-Other-Income.md).  


## Foreign Income - Box 15, and Foreign Tax Paid - Box 16

Debit Box 15 amount − Box 16 amount (net): `Deposits - investment` (1002-2).  
Debit Box 16 amount: `Withholding taxes` (9283).  
Credit Box 15 amount: `Dividends from foreign sources` (8097).  
If declared in December but paid in January, the first debit goes to `Investment distributions receivable` (1060-1).  

Schedule entries:
- Schedule 7 (S7) / Part 1 / Box 032 (worldwide income from property): the Box 15 amount, net of any expenses
  attributable to that source
  - Feeds aggregate investment income (line 092 → T2 line 440); the entry most easily missed
- S7 / Part 3 / Box 019 (foreign-source income from property, net of related expenses): the same net figure
  - The foreign subset → line 445
  - "Net of related expenses" is operative: with no directly attributable costs — the usual case for a portfolio
    holding — net equals gross and the slip amount goes in unchanged
  - Where costs are attributable (counsel fees on the holding, interest on money borrowed to buy it), allocate them
    by source and file the net amount; do not also deduct them on Schedule 125
- Schedule 21 (S21) / Part 1 (federal foreign non-business income tax credit): claim the Box 16 amount
  - Deducts it from Canadian tax otherwise payable (ITA [s.126(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-126.html))
- Schedule 1 (S1) / Page 3 / Other additions (Description 605; Amount 295): add back the Box 16 amount
  - Book it as a "Foreign tax add-back" so the tax expensed in 9283 is not also claimed as a credit (no double-dipping)

This mirrors [T3 foreign income - Box 25/34](../T3/T3.md#foreign-income---box-25-and-foreign-tax-withheld---box-34), with one difference to watch:
- On a broker-issued T5, the foreign income in Box 15 is not necessarily a dividend
  - Directly held foreign bonds or T-bills produce foreign *interest*
- When the underlying is foreign interest, credit an interest-style account rather than `Dividends from foreign sources`
  - Use the foreign interest / other-property line of the S7 worksheet rather than the foreign-dividend line
- The S21 foreign tax credit math is the same either way

If Box 27 shows a foreign currency, convert each box to CAD at the payment-date rate before posting.  
See [Foreign Currency](../../Bookkeeping/Foreign-Currency/Foreign-Currency.md).  


## Royalties - Box 17

Debit: `Deposits - investment` (1002-2).  
Credit: `Other investment income` (8090-2), or a dedicated royalty sub-account if you earn them regularly.  

For a portfolio holder, royalties are property income and form part of AII on Schedule 7, the same as Box 14.  
Royalties from an active royalty business would instead be active business income (outside the scope of this page).  


## Capital Gains Dividends - Box 18

A capital gains dividend is paid by a mutual fund corporation.  
It is a deemed capital gain, not a taxable dividend: no s.112 deduction and no Part IV tax apply.  
It is reported on Schedule 6, half of it is taxable, and the non-taxable half is added to the Capital Dividend Account.  
See [T5 - Box 18 Capital Gains Dividends](T5-Box-18-Capital-Gains-Dividends.md) for the full treatment and the contrast with T3 Box 21 and T5008.  


## Worked Example

A corporate account holds a high-interest savings (ISA) deposit and a Canadian dividend stock.  
It also holds a US dividend stock and a corporate-class fund.  
The year's T5 shows: Box 13 = $400, Box 24 = $300, Box 15 = $200, Box 16 = $30, Box 18 = $500.  
All amounts are received in cash during the year.  

Box 13 interest, $400:  
Debit `Deposits - investment` (1002-2) = $400  
Credit `Interest from other Canadian sources` (8094) = $400  
→ S7 property income (AII)  

Box 24 eligible dividend, $300:  
Debit `Deposits - investment` (1002-2) = $300  
Credit `Dividends from Canadian sources` (8096) = $300  
→ S3 Part 1, s.112 deduction $300, Part IV 38⅓% = $115 → ERDTOH  

Box 15/16 foreign income $200, foreign tax $30:  
Debit `Deposits - investment` (1002-2) = $170  
Debit `Withholding taxes` (9283) = $30  
Credit `Dividends from foreign sources` (8097) = $200  
→ S7 Part 1 Box 032 = $200 and Part 3 Box 019 = $200, S21 FTC = $30, S1 line 605 add-back = $30  

Box 18 capital gains dividend, $500:  
Debit `Deposits - investment` (1002-2) = $500  
Credit `Capital gains dividends` (8211-3) = $500  
→ S6 reports a $500 gain; taxable half $250 → S1 line 113 (auto) and AII  
→ S1 line 395 deduction = $500 (the full amount booked to 8211-3); CDA += $250  

Cash into `Deposits - investment` (1002-2): $400 + $300 + $170 + $500 = $1,370.
