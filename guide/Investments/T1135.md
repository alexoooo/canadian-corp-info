# T1135 Foreign Income Verification Statement

**Who this is for**:
- Owners of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC)
- Holding US-listed stocks or ETFs (or other foreign property) in the corporate brokerage account

**TLDR**:
- The corporation must file Form T1135 for any year in which its *specified foreign property* had a total *cost amount* over $100,000 CAD at any time
- US-listed stocks and ETFs count even when held at a Canadian broker; Canadian-listed ETFs that hold US stocks do not
- The threshold runs on cost (the ACB), not market value, so the [ACB tracker](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base-Tracking.md) already holds the number
- T1135 is an information return: it reports holdings, not tax, and is required even when every dollar of the income is already on the T2
- Late filing costs $25 per day ($100 minimum, $2,500 maximum), with no tax owing needed to trigger it

Limitations:
- Scope is a CCPC whose foreign property is portfolio securities in a brokerage account; foreign rental real estate is identified but not worked through
- *Foreign affiliates* (a 10%-or-more owned non-resident corporation) are out of scope: they are excluded from T1135 and reported on Form T1134 instead
- Form T106 (non-arm's-length transactions with non-residents) is out of scope
- The personal T1135 (an individual over the same threshold) follows the same rules but is out of scope
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## Filing obligation

A *specified Canadian entity* must file Form T1135 for a tax year when the total cost amount of its *specified foreign property* exceeds $100,000 CAD at any time in that year (ITA [s.233.3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-233.html)).  
A CCPC is a specified Canadian entity; the exclusions (mutual fund corporations, certain exempt entities) do not apply to an owner-managed corporation.  

Mechanics of the test:
- *At any time in the year*: crossing $100,000 for a single day triggers the filing, even if the property was sold before year-end
- The threshold is the *total* across all specified foreign property, not per security or per account
- Due date: the corporation's T2 filing-due date, 6 months after year-end (s.233.3(3))
- The T2 return asks whether a T1135 is required; answer it consistently with the filing

Filing the T1135 does not change the tax:
- The income was already taxed through the T2 (the T3/T5 foreign-income boxes, the T5008 gains)
- The form exists so CRA can verify that foreign holdings and their income line up


## What counts as specified foreign property

*Specified foreign property* (defined in s.233.3(1)) covers funds and intangible property held outside Canada, shares and debt of non-residents, and foreign real property.  
Where the property is *held* mostly does not matter; who *issued* it does.  

| Holding | SFP | Why |
|---|---|---|
| US-listed stock (`AAPL`) at a Canadian broker | yes | share of a non-resident corporation; the broker's location is irrelevant |
| US-listed ETF (`VTI`) | yes | non-resident issuer (typically treated as a corporation for Canadian tax) |
| Canadian-listed ETF holding US stocks (`VUN`, `XUU`) | no | the fund itself is a Canadian-resident trust |
| `DLR` / `DLR.U` (TSX-listed) | no | Canadian-resident trust, whatever the trading currency |
| USD cash at a Canadian bank or broker | no | funds deposited in Canada |
| USD cash at a US bank | yes | funds held outside Canada |
| Bond or T-bill of a non-resident issuer | yes | debt owed by a non-resident |
| US rental real estate | yes | real property outside Canada, unless used exclusively in an active business |
| Shares of a foreign affiliate | no | reported on Form T1134 instead |
| Canadian shares, GICs, bonds, mutual funds | no | not foreign property |

Property used exclusively in an active business is excluded, whatever its location.  
Cryptocurrency held on a non-resident exchange may be specified foreign property as intangible property situated outside Canada; CRA's position is still developing, so verify current guidance before relying on either answer.  


## Cost amount and the threshold test

The threshold and most of the form run on *cost amount* (ITA [s.248(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-248.html)), which for portfolio securities is the [adjusted cost base](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base.md).  
Market value is irrelevant to the $100,000 test: a position bought for $90,000 that doubles does not trigger a filing; one bought for $110,000 that halves does.  

The cost amount is measured in CAD:
- Trade-date FX for purchases, matching the [ACB conventions](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base.md#foreign-assets-and-fx-conversion-to-cad)
- The per-security `Total ACB` column of the [ACB tracker](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base-Tracking.md) is the threshold input: sum it over the securities that are SFP
- A security's cost amount does not fluctuate with the exchange rate after purchase; only transactions move it

Maintain the SFP subset as a flag in the [security master](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base-Tracking.md#investment-identification---security-master): mark each security yes/no once, when it is first purchased.  


## Reporting methods

The form has two parts; which one applies depends on the year's peak total cost.  

*Simplified method* (Part A), available when the total cost stayed under $250,000 throughout the year:
- Tick the types of property held
- Name the top three countries by cost amount
- Report the total income from all specified foreign property, and the total gain or loss on dispositions
- No per-property detail

*Detailed method* (Part B), required once the total cost reaches $250,000 at any time:
- Report per property, sorted into seven categories (funds outside Canada, non-resident shares, non-resident debt, non-resident trusts, foreign real property, other, and securities-dealer accounts)
- For most categories: country, maximum cost amount during the year, cost amount at year-end, income, and gain or loss on disposition

*Category 7* is the practical path for a corporate brokerage account:
- All specified foreign property held in accounts with a *Canadian registered securities dealer* may be reported in category 7, aggregated country by country, instead of security by security
- Category 7 uses *fair market value*, not cost: the highest month-end FMV during the year and the FMV at year-end
- The month-end FMV comes straight off the brokerage statements; keep the twelve statements with the year's records

Income and gain figures tie back to the books:
- Income: the foreign-income ledger accounts behind [T3 Box 25](T3/T3_Box-25-Foreign-Income_Box-34-Foreign-Tax-Withheld.md) and [T5 Box 15](T5/T5.md#foreign-income---box-15-and-foreign-tax-paid---box-16)
- Gains and losses: dispositions of the SFP subset from the [T5008 workflow](T5008/T5008.md)


## Worked example

An Ontario CCPC's brokerage account holds, all year:
- `VTI` (US-listed): ACB US$80,000, booked at CAD $105,000 trade-date FX
- `VUN` (TSX-listed): ACB CAD $60,000
- USD cash at the Canadian broker: CAD $20,000

The SFP test:
- Only `VTI` is specified foreign property → total SFP cost amount $105,000
- $105,000 > $100,000 → a T1135 must be filed with the T2
- The total stayed under $250,000 all year → the simplified method (Part A) is available: tick "shares of non-resident corporations", country United States, and report the year's `VTI` dividends and any disposition gain

If the corporation later adds US$120,000 of `VTI` (total SFP cost ≈ $270,000):
- The $250,000 line is crossed → Part B applies from that year on
- Report category 7, country United States, highest month-end FMV and year-end FMV from the brokerage statements


## Penalties and missed filings

- *Late filing*: $25 per day, $100 minimum, $2,500 maximum per year (ITA [s.162(7)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-162.html)); no unpaid tax is needed — a nil-income year still accrues it
- *Knowing or grossly negligent failure*: $500 per month up to 24 months (ITA [s.162(10)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-162.html))
- *Extended reassessment*: the normal reassessment period for the year extends by three years when the T1135 was not filed on time and foreign-property income was omitted (ITA [s.152(4)(b.2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-152.html))

Multiple missed years can be corrected through CRA's *Voluntary Disclosures Program* before CRA makes contact; penalty relief is discretionary.  
A first missed year discovered while preparing the current T2 is worth professional advice: filing late triggers the s.162(7) penalty automatically, while a VDP application can cover it.
