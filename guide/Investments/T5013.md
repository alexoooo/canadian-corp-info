# T5013

**Who this is for**:
- Owners of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) whose corporate brokerage account holds partnership units
  - A publicly traded limited partnership, an income fund structured as an LP, or a private partnership interest
- Turning the *T5013 Statement of Partnership Income* into ledger entries, ACB adjustments, and T2 schedule inputs

**TLDR**:
- A partnership allocates its income to the partners whether or not it distributes cash; the T5013 reports your share
- The income keeps its character as it flows through: interest stays interest, dividends stay dividends, capital gains stay capital gains
- Cash distributions are not income; they reduce the interest's ACB, and allocated income increases it
  - Two moving parts per year, both tracked in the ACB record
- The slip usually arrives late in slip season; the amounts follow the partnership's fiscal period, not the calendar

Limitations:
- Scope is a passive corporate holder of partnership units in a brokerage account
- Business income allocated by a partnership drags in the specified-partnership-income rules for the SBD; that is professional-advice territory and out of scope
- Tax shelters, at-risk-amount limitations on claiming losses, and tiered partnerships are out of scope
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## When the Slip Arrives

Holdings that produce a T5013 instead of (or beside) a T3 or T5:
- Units of a publicly traded limited partnership (many energy, infrastructure, and asset-manager vehicles)
- An income fund or ETF structured as a partnership rather than a trust
- Any private partnership the corporation has joined

The partnership computes income at its own fiscal-period end and allocates each partner a share (ITA [s.96(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-96.html)).  
The allocation is taxable to the partner for the taxation year in which the partnership's fiscal period ends, cash or no cash.  
A distribution paid during the year is a separate event: a draw against capital, not income.  
This is the reverse of the T3/T5 world, where cash and income usually travel together; see [Investments](Investments.md).  


## Character Flow-Through

Each income type on the slip lands where it would have landed if earned directly:
- *Interest and foreign income*: property income, into aggregate investment income on Schedule 7
- *Canadian dividends*: the s.112 deduction and Part IV tax apply as if the corporation received the dividend itself (see TODO)
  - Reported through Schedule 3 like a directly received dividend
- *Capital gains*: onto Schedule 6, half taxable, with the untaxed half feeding the [CDA](Capital-Dividend-Account/Capital-Dividend-Account.md)
- *Rental income*: property income, into Schedule 7
- *Business income*: allocated business income is outside this page's scope (the specified-partnership-income rules)

For the AII consequences — NERDTOH and the SBD grind — see [T3 Box 26](T3/T3-Box-26-Other-Income.md).  


## The Boxes

The boxes an owner-managed corporate holder actually meets (verify the layout against the slip in hand; see TODO):

| Box | What it reports | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| 105 | Limited partner's at-risk amount | informational; caps loss claims |
| 110 | Canadian and foreign net rental income | Schedule 7 |
| 113 | Return of capital | no income; reduces ACB |
| 128 | Interest from Canadian sources | Schedule 7 |
| 132 | Actual amount of eligible dividends | Schedule 3; a corporation uses the actual amount, not the grossed-up box |
| 135 | Foreign dividend and interest income | Schedule 7; any foreign tax withheld feeds Schedule 21 |
| 151 | Capital gains (losses) | Schedule 6 |
| 210 | Total carrying charges | deductible against the allocated income |

The gross-up and dividend-tax-credit boxes beside 132 are personal-return machinery; a corporation ignores them.  


## Bookkeeping

Book the allocation and the distribution as separate entries, mirroring the ACB moves.  

The year's allocation (from the slip), say $800 interest and $1,200 capital gain:
- Debit `Long-term investments - partnership units` (a `2300`-area sub-account) = $2,000
- Credit `Interest from other Canadian sources` (8094) = $800
- Credit `Realized gains/losses on sale of investments` (8211) = $1,200

Cash distributions received during the year, say $1,500:
- Debit `Deposits - investment` (1002-2) = $1,500
- Credit `Long-term investments - partnership units` = $1,500

The investment account then tracks the ACB: cost, plus allocated income, minus distributions.  
Keep the per-unit detail in the ACB spreadsheet; see [Adjusted Cost Base Tracking](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base-Tracking.md).  


## ACB of the Partnership Interest

The interest's ACB moves twice a year, in opposite directions:
- *Up* by the allocated income, for fiscal periods ending before the measurement time (ITA [s.53(1)(e)(i)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-53.html))
  - The addition lands after the fiscal-period end — sell mid-period and the final allocation still adjusts the ACB used on the sale
- *Down* by distributions of profits or capital received, and by allocated losses (ITA [s.53(2)(c)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-53.html))

A limited partner whose ACB goes negative has a deemed capital gain of the negative amount (ITA [s.40(3.1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-40.html)).  
High-distribution LPs reach this over a long hold; the ACB record is what warns you.  
On an eventual sale, the broker's book cost will not reflect these adjustments — the corporation's own ACB record governs; see [Adjusted Cost Base](Adjusted-Cost-Base/Adjusted-Cost-Base.md#book-cost-vs-acb).
