# Home Office and the Principal Residence

**Who this is for**:
- CCPC owner-employees weighing the personal routes for home-workspace costs
- Selling a home that housed an office, expecting the reporting and the exemption

**TLDR**:
- An employee required to work from home and not reimbursed deducts workspace costs via forms T2200 and T777
  - Mortgage interest and CCA are never deductible on this route; the corporate reimbursement is usually simpler
- The family home's gain is sheltered by the *principal residence exemption* (PRE)
  - The sale must still be reported, on Schedule 3 and Form T2091, or the exemption is at risk
- Claiming CCA on a home-office portion endangers the exemption; every route shares this trap
- An ancillary office with no structural change and no CCA leaves the exemption intact

Limitations:
- This page is the personal side of the home's costs
  - The corporate reimbursement and rent routes are worked in [Owner-Corporation Transactions](../Paying-Yourself/Owner-Corporation-Transactions.md#home-office)
- Rental property beyond the home-office slice is out of scope; see [Rental and Property Income](../Operations/Rental-And-Property-Income.md)
- The s.45(2) and s.45(3) change-in-use elections are named, not worked
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## The Employee Home-Office Deduction

An owner-manager is an employee of the corporation, so the employee rules can apply to them personally.  
The route exists for an employee *required* to maintain a workspace and *not* reimbursed for it.  

The gate (ITA [s.8(1)(i)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-8.html), limited by s.8(13)) needs one of two branches:
- The workspace is where you principally perform the employment duties
- It is used exclusively to earn employment income, and regularly and continuously for meeting clients

The paperwork and the limits:
- The employer certifies the conditions on form T2200; the claim goes on form T777
  - The temporary flat-rate method ended after 2022; only the detailed method remains
- The deduction cannot exceed the employment income; the excess carries forward against the same employer
- A salaried employee claims the workspace share of utilities, rent, maintenance, and reasonable internet
  - Never mortgage interest, mortgage principal, property tax, home insurance, or CCA
- A commission employee adds home insurance and property tax, but still no mortgage interest or CCA

For an owner-manager this route is the weakest of the three:
- The corporate reimbursement covers the same running costs without touching the personal return
- The sole proprietor's s.18(12) claim, the widest, is worked in [T2125 and Expenses](../Sole-Proprietorship/T2125-And-Expenses.md#business-use-of-home)


## The Principal Residence Exemption

The PRE shelters the gain on a home for the years it is designated (ITA [s.40(2)(b)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-40.html)):
- The exempt fraction is (1 + designated years) ÷ years owned; the plus-one absorbs a year of overlap when moving homes
- A *principal residence* must be ordinarily inhabited in the year by you, your spouse, or a child (ITA [s.54](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-54.html))
- One property per family unit per year: spouses and minor children share a single designation

The reporting, mandatory since 2016:
- Every sale of a principal residence goes on Schedule 3 (T1) with a Form T2091 designation
  - Fully exempt sales complete only T2091's first page
- An unreported sale risks the exemption itself, and leaves the year open to reassessment
- CRA accepts a late designation at its discretion, at $100 per month late up to $8,000


## Change of Use and the CCA Trap

Converting part of the home to income-producing use can trigger a deemed disposition at fair market value
(ITA [s.45(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-45.html)), and the converted portion stops accruing exempt years.  

CRA does not apply the change-in-use rules to a home office when all three conditions hold
(Folio S1-F3-C2, para 2.59):
- The income-producing use is *ancillary* to the home's main use as a residence
- There is no structural change to the property
- No CCA is claimed on the home

This is why [Owner-Corporation Transactions](../Paying-Yourself/Owner-Corporation-Transactions.md#home-office)
and [T2125 and Expenses](../Sole-Proprietorship/T2125-And-Expenses.md#business-use-of-home) both warn against CCA:
- Running costs (utilities, maintenance) stay claimable without consequence for the exemption
- CCA is not what causes the income-producing use — the use itself did that, when the space started earning income
  - What CCA does is forfeit the administrative concession above, which is the only thing keeping the partial-change
    rule switched off
  - CRA can then apply that rule from the earlier date the use began, not from the year the CCA was claimed
- The few dollars of CCA are never worth exposing a share of the home's gain to tax

For a full conversion (the home becomes a rental, or a rental becomes the home), elections can defer
the deemed disposition: s.45(2) on the way out, s.45(3) on the way back.  
Both are named here only; s.45(4) bars the s.45(3) election where CCA was ever claimed.
