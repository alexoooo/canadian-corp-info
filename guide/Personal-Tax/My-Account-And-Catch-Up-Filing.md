# My Account and Catch-Up Filing

**Who this is for**:
- Owners who let personal filings lapse and want CRA account access and compliance back
- Registering for CRA's online services for the first time

**TLDR**:
- One *CRA account* credential now reaches My Account (personal), My Business Account, and Represent a Client
- New registration requires a return assessed within the last two years
  - The exception: signing in through a provincial partner (BC Services Card, Alberta.ca Account)

## The CRA Account

CRA consolidated its sign-ins in 2025: one credential, one front door, three portals behind it.  
*My Account* is the personal-tax portal, the mirror of the corporate [My Business Account](../Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md#types-of-accounts).  

Sign-in options:
- A CRA user ID and password
- A *Sign-In Partner*: your online-banking credential
- A provincial digital ID: BC Services Card or Alberta.ca Account

What My Account holds for the situations on this page: assessed returns and NOAs, slip copies,
RRSP and TFSA room as CRA has computed it, uncashed cheques, and the *Change my return* and relief-request services.  


## Registration and Recovery After a Lapse

Registration verifies your identity against your tax history:
- The flow asks for figures from your most recent assessed return (current or previous year)
- No return assessed in the last two years means registration is blocked until you file
  - The provincial-partner sign-in (BC, Alberta) is the stated exception, needing only the SIN
- Identity confirmation is a choice of two:
  - *Document verification service*: a real-time photo of yourself and a government photo ID, full access immediately
  - A *CRA security code* mailed to the address on file, up to 10 business days

The practical order for a lapsed filer outside BC and Alberta is therefore file first, register after:
- Prepare the most recent year from slips and records and file it (on paper if NETFILE access is the problem)
- Once it is assessed, register with the document verification service
- With access restored, Auto-fill and the CRA slip copies make the remaining back years faster

An existing CRA account does not get revoked for non-filing; the data behind it just goes stale.  


## Filing Back Years

- NETFILE accepts initial returns back to 2018 (as of the 2026 season); older years go on paper or through a preparer
- File oldest-first: carryforwards (RRSP room, losses, donation carryforwards) build in sequence
- The ordinary refund rule is *three* years: ITA [s.164(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-164.html) requires the return within three years of the year-end
- Beyond three years, an individual relies on extended relief: under s.164(1.5)(a) the Minister **may** refund an
  overpayment where the return was filed within 10 calendar years after the year-end
  - "May", not "must" — it is discretionary relief, not an entitlement, so an old refund is worth pursuing but
    should not be counted on
  - Outside the 10-year window the discretion is gone entirely; balance-owing years never expire
- Benefits keyed to the T1 (GST/HST credit, CCB) resume only once the missing returns are assessed


## Penalties and Interest on Late Years

- *Late-filing penalty*: 5% of the balance owing plus 1% per complete month late, to a maximum of 12 months
  (ITA [s.162(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-162.html))
  - After a demand to file and a penalty in the prior three years, the rates double and run to 20 months (s.162(2))
- *Arrears interest*: prescribed rate plus 4%, compounded daily from the balance-due day
- A year with no balance owing has no late-filing penalty; the cost of not filing it is the stalled refund and benefits


## Relief and Voluntary Disclosure

*Taxpayer relief* (ITA [s.220(3.1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-220.html)):
- CRA can cancel penalties and interest (never the tax) up to 10 years back, on Form RC4288 or through the CRA account
- Grounds: CRA error or delay, circumstances beyond your control, or inability to pay

*Voluntary Disclosures Program* (VDP): unreported income or unfiled years disclosed before CRA comes asking:
- An *unprompted* application gets full penalty relief plus 75% interest relief
  - A *prompted* one gets up to full penalty relief plus 25% interest relief
- Applications go on Form RC199; an ongoing audit of the issue bars the application

Both programs are the same machinery the corporation uses.  
The classing rules and the 2025 program overhaul are worked in
[CRA Administration](../Filing-And-CRA/CRA-Administration.md#relief-programs); for anything material, involve a professional first.
