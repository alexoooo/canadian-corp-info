# TFSA and RRSP Contributions

**Who this is for**:
- CCPC owners contributing personally to a TFSA or RRSP out of salary or dividends
- Fixing an over-contribution, or expecting the tax and forms one brings

**TLDR**:
- TFSA room accrues to every adult resident regardless of income: $7,000 for 2026, $109,000 cumulative since 2009
- RRSP room accrues at 18% of prior-year *earned income*; salary creates it, dividends do not
- An RRSP contribution deducts for a year if made by 60 days after that year-end
- TFSA excess: 1% per month on the highest excess amount, reported on Form RC243 by June 30 of the next year
  - The fix is withdrawing the excess; a *reasonable error* corrected without delay can be waived
- A TFSA withdrawal restores room only on the following January 1, never in-year
- RRSP excess beyond a $2,000 buffer: 1% per month, reported on a T1-OVP within 90 days of year-end

Limitations:
- FHSA, RESP, spousal RRSPs and attribution, the Home Buyers' and Lifelong Learning Plans, and RRIFs are out of scope
- What to hold inside the plans is out of scope; the corporate account's investing is worked in [Investments](../Investments/Investments.md)
- Dollar limits are the 2026 amounts; CRA resets them annually
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## Contribution Room Basics

*TFSA*: room accrues from the year you turn 18 (2009 at the earliest), filing or not:
- The annual limit is $7,000 for 2026; someone 18+ in 2009 who never contributed holds $109,000
- Contributions are not deductible, and growth and withdrawals are tax-free
- The room figure in the [CRA account](My-Account-And-Catch-Up-Filing.md) updates from issuer reports
  - Issuers report annually, so the figure lags your current-year activity; keep your own running total

*RRSP*: room accrues at 18% of the prior year's earned income, up to the dollar limit ($33,810 for 2026):
- Only *earned income* counts: salary from the corporation qualifies, dividends never do
  - Sizing salary to the room target is worked in [Salary vs Dividends](../Paying-Yourself/Salary-Vs-Dividends.md#rrsp-room)
- A contribution deducts for a year if made in the year or within 60 days after its end
  (ITA [s.146(5)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-146.html))
  - For the 2025 year the cut-off was March 2, 2026
- Contributing and deducting are separate acts: an undeducted contribution can wait for a higher-income year
  - It still consumes room the moment it is made, which is what the over-contribution rules measure


## TFSA Over-Contribution

Contributing past your room triggers a monthly tax (ITA [s.207.02](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-207.02.html)):
- 1% of the *highest* excess amount in each month the excess exists
- The year's tax is reported on Form RC243, due with payment by June 30 of the following year
- The fix is withdrawing the excess immediately; the tax stops accruing the month after the excess is gone

CRA can waive the tax (ITA [s.207.06](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-207.06.html)):
- The excess must have arisen from a *reasonable error*
- The excess and its attributable income must be withdrawn without delay
- Waiver is discretionary; respond to CRA's excess letter rather than waiting out the tax


## The Same-Year Recontribution Trap

A withdrawal is added back to room on January 1 of the *following* year
(the "unused TFSA contribution room" definition, ITA [s.207.01(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-207.01.html)).  
Withdrawing and recontributing in the same year therefore double-counts against this year's room.  

With no unused room carried into 2026:
- January: contribute the year's $7,000 limit → room now $0
- March: withdraw $5,000 for an expense → room still $0; the $5,000 comes back on 2027-01-01
- November: recontribute the $5,000 → a $5,000 excess, taxed at 1% per month
- Waiting until January 2027 to recontribute would have cost nothing

The trap is treating the TFSA like a chequing account.  
Money that will go back in before year-end is better parked outside the plan.  


## RRSP Over-Contribution

Contributions beyond your deduction limit are tolerated up to a $2,000 lifetime buffer
(ITA [s.204.2(1.1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-204.2.html)):
- The buffer exists only for someone who was 18 or older in a prior year
- A buffer amount is not deductible now; it absorbs into new room as room accrues

Beyond the buffer, Part X.1 tax runs (ITA [s.204.1(2.1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-204.1.html)):
- 1% of the cumulative excess per month
- Reported on a T1-OVP return, due with payment within 90 days after the calendar year-end
- The fix is withdrawing the excess; Form T3012A lets the issuer refund it without withholding tax
  - Without the form, the issuer withholds, and the withheld amount reconciles on the T1

The common cause is contributing against the current year's salary instead of the prior year's room.  
Check the room figure on the latest notice of assessment before a large contribution, not after.
