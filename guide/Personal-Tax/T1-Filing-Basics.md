# T1 Filing Basics

**Who this is for**:
- CCPC owners handling their own T1 in an ordinary year
- Following the slips the corporation issues (T4, T5) to where they land personally

**TLDR**:
- Filing is due April 30, or June 15 when you or your spouse is self-employed
  - The balance is due April 30 either way
- Slips arrive by the end of February (T3s by the end of March); Auto-fill pulls CRA's copies into your software
- Your income is what your records say, whether or not a slip arrives
- File through NETFILE-certified software; reconcile the notice of assessment against what you filed
- Net tax owing over $3,000 in the current year and a recent prior year brings quarterly instalments

Limitations:
- Basic common situations only; T1 brackets and the full credit list are out of scope
- Quebec differences are out of scope
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## Deadlines and the Balance-Due Day

- Filing: April 30 of the following year (ITA [s.150(1)(d)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-150.html))
  - June 15 when you or your cohabiting spouse or common-law partner carried on a business
- Payment: the *balance-due day* is April 30 regardless (ITA [s.248(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-248.html))
  - Interest runs from May 1 on anything unpaid

The June 15 extension and its self-employment trigger are worked in
[CPP and the T1](../Sole-Proprietorship/CPP-And-The-T1.md#the-june-15-deadline).  

Instalments enter once *net tax owing* tops $3,000 in the current year and at least one of the two prior years
(ITA [s.156.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-156.1.html)):
- Dividends carry no withholding, so a dividend-paid owner crosses the threshold the same way a sole proprietor does
- The due dates and computation options are worked in [CPP and the T1](../Sole-Proprietorship/CPP-And-The-T1.md#t1-instalments)


## Slips and Auto-Fill My Return

The slip set a CCPC owner typically sees:
- T4 for salary and T5 for dividends, both issued by the corporation itself
  - The issuing side is worked in [Payroll](../Paying-Yourself/Payroll.md) and [Bookkeeping and Slips](../Paying-Yourself/Dividends/Bookkeeping-And-Slips.md)
- T3, T5, and T5008 from any personal brokerage account
- RRSP contribution receipts from the plan issuer

Issuer deadlines: most slips by the end of February; T3s by the end of March.  
Filing before the T3s arrive invites an amendment, so a personal ETF holder waits for April.  

### T4 and T5 to the T1

The corporation's two owner slips land on specific lines. Certified software fills these from the slip, but the map
is what lets you check it:

| Slip box | What it holds | T1 line |
|---|---|---|
| T4 box 14 | Employment income (salary plus taxable benefits) | 10100 |
| T4 box 22 | Income tax deducted | 43700 |
| T4 box 16 / 16A | Employee CPP / CPP2 contributions | base portion at 30800; enhanced portion deducted at 22215 |
| T5 box 11 | *Taxable* amount of dividends other than eligible | 12010, and into 12000 |
| T5 box 25 | *Taxable* amount of eligible dividends | 12000 |
| T5 box 12 + box 26 | Federal dividend tax credit | 40425 |

The trap is the dividend boxes: **boxes 10 and 24 are the actual dividends paid, and are not what goes on the
return**. Boxes 11 and 25 are those amounts already grossed up, and they are the income figures.  
Entering the cash instead of the grossed-up amount understates income and, worse, leaves the dividend tax credit
unclaimed — the credit only makes sense against the grossed-up figure.  

The Ontario dividend tax credit is calculated on the provincial form from the same taxable amounts; the gross-up and
credit mechanics are worked in [Tax Integration](../Overview/Tax-Integration.md).  
For the issuing side — how the corporation produces these slips — see [Payroll](../Paying-Yourself/Payroll.md) and
[Bookkeeping and Slips](../Paying-Yourself/Dividends/Bookkeeping-And-Slips.md).  
Brokerage slips (T3, T5008) are covered in [Investments](../Investments/Investments.md); they are not duplicated here.  

*Auto-fill my return* downloads CRA's slip copies into certified software:
- It needs a [CRA account](My-Account-And-Catch-Up-Filing.md) and serves the current year and several prior years
- It also carries RRSP room and carryover balances
- Reconcile the download against your own records rather than accepting it
  - A slip can be late, amended, or missing from CRA's copy at filing time


## A Missing Slip Is Still Income

The T1 is self-assessed: you report income from your records, and slips are the cross-check, not the definition.  
CRA matches its slip copies against the filed return and reassesses unreported amounts it finds.  

- A slip that never arrives does not make the income untaxed; report it from statements
- A slip that arrives after filing is handled by amending (below), not by a fresh return
- The same principle for a sole proprietor's T4A is worked in [Becoming a Sole Proprietor](../Sole-Proprietorship/Becoming-A-Sole-Proprietor.md#the-t4a-you-receive)


## NETFILE and the Notice of Assessment

- File through NETFILE-certified software; the 2026 season accepts initial returns for 2018 through 2025
- The *notice of assessment* (NOA) usually accepts the return as filed
  - Compare it line by line; a changed line comes with an explanation paragraph
- Amending an assessed return: *ReFILE* in the same software, *Change my return* in the CRA account, or Form T1-ADJ

The corporate analogue of the assessment cycle, objections included, is worked in
[CRA Administration](../Filing-And-CRA/CRA-Administration.md).
