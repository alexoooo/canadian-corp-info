# Salary vs Dividends

**Who this is for**:
- Owners of a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) deciding how to take a specific year's compensation
- Already oriented on [Tax Integration](../Overview/Tax-Integration.md) (why the headline tax lands close either way)

**TLDR**:
- Integration makes the *total* tax similar; the decision is made by the secondary lines:
  - CPP: a real cash cost now (both halves), buying pension entitlement later — salary only
  - RRSP room: 18% of salary up to the year's dollar limit — salary only
  - SBD management: a bonus is the standard tool to keep corporate income at the business limit
  - GRIP: profit left at the general rate builds eligible-dividend capacity
- Some years the answer is decided outright: income over the business limit, no GRIP, an RRSP target, or a preference for zero payroll admin
- The levers combine: a fixed salary plus a dividend top-up is the common owner-manager pattern, not either-or

Limitations:
- WSIB and income splitting stay out of scope (see [Further Reading](../Overview/Further-Reading.md)); the comparison works without them
- Personal T1 arithmetic is shown structurally; verify the year's brackets and credits before relying on any figure
- TOSI constraints on paying family members are the [Dividends](Dividends/Dividends.md) page's territory
- The following is my understanding as of 2026


## The Two Routes for the Same Dollar

$100,000 of small-business-rate profit the owner wants out, Ontario:

| | Salary route | Dividend route |
|---|---|---|
| Corporation deducts | $100,000 (plus employer CPP) | nothing |
| Corporate tax | $0 on the paid-out amount | ≈ 12.2% → $12,200 (see note) |
| Cash to the owner | $100,000 gross | $87,800 as a non-eligible dividend |
| Owner's T1 | employment income, at full marginal rates | grossed up 15%, then the dividend tax credit |
| Owner also pays | employee CPP; corp pays employer CPP | no CPP |
| Slip | T4 | T5 |

The corporate rate note: the SBD-rate figure moves with the province; Ontario's cut effective 2026-07-01 lowers the combined rate — see [Small Business Tax Overview](../Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md#what-is-corporate-tax) for the current numbers.  
Integration is calibrated so the two columns' *combined* corporate-plus-personal tax finishes within a few percentage points of each other; the per-flavour arithmetic is worked on [Tax Integration](../Overview/Tax-Integration.md) and [Dividends - worked examples](Dividends/Dividends-Examples.md).  
What follows is the lines that actually differ.  


## CPP: Cost Now, Entitlement Later

Salary triggers CPP on both sides of the payroll; dividends trigger none.  

2026 figures (they move every January; see [Payroll](Payroll.md)):
- Base CPP: 5.95% employee + 5.95% employer on pensionable earnings between $3,500 and the $74,600 YMPE
  - Maximum $4,230.45 each half — about $8,461 combined at or above the YMPE
- CPP2: 4% each on earnings between the YMPE and the $85,000 YAMPE, up to $416 each half

Reading the ~$9,300 combined maximum:
- As a cost: real cash leaving the corporation-plus-owner unit every salary year
- As a purchase: contributory years toward the CPP retirement pension, disability coverage, and survivor benefits
- Which reading dominates depends on age, years already contributed, and confidence in self-directed saving
  - A dividend-only owner is opting out of CPP accrual entirely; that is a retirement-planning decision, not a tax rounding


## RRSP Room

Only salary is *earned income* for RRSP purposes:
- Room accrues at 18% of the prior year's earned income, up to the dollar limit ($33,810 for 2026)
- Maximizing 2026 room takes about $187,800 of 2025 salary
- Dividends create no room at any amount

A salary pitched at the RRSP target is a common anchor:
- Pay enough salary to fund the intended RRSP contribution; take the rest as dividends
- The RRSP deduction then shelters part of the salary immediately


## SBD, AAII, and GRIP

The corporate-side levers the choice pulls:
- *Income at the business limit*: profit above $500,000 is taxed at the general rate
  - A bonus down to the limit is the standard response; the mechanics and the 180-day payment rule are in [Payroll - Owner-Manager Remuneration](Payroll.md#owner-manager-remuneration)
  - Leaving it at the general rate instead builds *GRIP* — eligible-dividend capacity at a better personal rate later
  - Neither answer is free; this is the levers-combining case, not a rule
- *An AAII grind in progress*: passive income over $50,000 shrinks the business limit itself
  - Salary reduces active income but does not cure the grind; profit squeezed out of the SBD band is taxed at the general rate and feeds GRIP
  - See [Small Business Tax Overview](../Overview/Small-Business-Tax.md#active-vs-investment-income)
- *Dividends drain the pools in order*: a capital dividend (tax-free, from CDA) beats both routes when the balance exists
  - Then non-eligible dividends releasing NERDTOH; see [ERDTOH and NERDTOH](Dividends/ERDTOH-NERDTOH.md)


## Cases Decided Outright

- *The owner needs RRSP room or CPP years*: salary, at least to the target level
- *No GRIP and a high personal bracket*: non-eligible dividends carry the worst integration gap; salary tends to win the comparison — run the year's numbers
- *Corporate income already over the business limit*: bonus down, unless deliberately building GRIP
- *CDA balance available*: the capital dividend first, whatever else the year takes
- *Simplicity above all*: dividends need no RP account, no monthly remittance, no T4 season
  - A resolution, the cash, and a T5 in February; see [Declaring and Paying](Dividends/Declaring-And-Paying.md)
- *A loss year*: salary in a loss year deepens the loss (deductible); dividends need retained earnings to be sensible, and paying them out of a loss position is a red flag for the balance sheet

The common pattern combines them: a fixed monthly salary sized to the RRSP/CPP target, plus a year-end dividend calibrated to the year's cash and pool positions.
