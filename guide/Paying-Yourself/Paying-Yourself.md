## Salary, Dividends, and the Shareholder Loan

Money earned inside a corporation is not yours until it comes out, and there are only a few ways to bring it out.  
This group covers each lever and the tradeoff between them.

The three levers:
- [Payroll](Payroll.md) covers paying yourself a salary: source deductions, remittances, and the T4
  - Salary is deductible to the corporation and creates RRSP room and CPP entitlement
- [Dividends](Dividends/Dividends.md) covers distributing after-tax profit
  - The three flavours: eligible, non-eligible, capital
  - Which corporate pool each flavour draws on, and how each is taxed in your hands
  - A hub with sub-pages for declaring, bookkeeping, T2 reporting, worked examples, and the ERDTOH/NERDTOH pools
- [Owner-corporation transactions](Owner-Corporation-Transactions.md) covers everything that is neither salary nor dividend
  - Mixed-use vehicles and home office, employee benefits, allowances and reimbursements, rent, and asset transfers
  - The shareholder loan account, plus the rules that keep each channel from becoming a taxable benefit

The salary-versus-dividend decision is where these meet.  
Because of [tax integration](../Overview/Tax-Integration.md), the headline tax cost is similar either way.  
The choice usually turns on CPP, RRSP room, cash-flow timing, and simplicity.
